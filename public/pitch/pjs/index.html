What we found

We read your website the way a hungry person does, then the way Google does.

Your site is not neglected. Elementor is patched to June, and somebody updated the specials page three days before we wrote this. The problem is not that nobody is looking after it. The problem is what it cannot do.

Fix this today

Your hours are not on your website.

We fetched all eleven pages. Zero of them publish an opening hour for either store. Not the homepage, not /contact/, not /contact-battle-creek/, not the Marshall menu, not the Battle Creek menu.

For a deli that opens at seven with a full breakfast board and closes at seven, and a lunch counter that runs ten to three on weekdays, that is the single most expensive gap on the site. It is also the question every phone search is asking.

Your contact page · Your Battle Creek page
Fix this today

Google is quoting your hot pastrami at $10.50.

Because your menus publish no prices, a third party filled the gap. allmenus.com carries a complete, fully priced, obsolete Pastrami Joe’s menu, and it ranks. It lists Joe’s Hot Pastrami at $10.50. A February 2025 review put the real price at “slightly over $13”.

That page also lists seven sandwiches you no longer make, a whole take-out market section, and a phone number that is not yours: (269) 248-7172.

Now read your reviews. The most consistent complaint about Pastrami Joe’s is price. People are being quoted ten fifty and charged thirteen, and then they write about it.

Fix this today

Battle Creek reads as closed, at an address you left in 2021.

There are two Yelp listings for Battle Creek. One is the current store. The other is 80 W. Michigan Ave., flagged CLOSED, holding thirty-one reviews and seventeen photos of a place you rebuilt somewhere else.

Tripadvisor is worse. Its only Battle Creek entry is still at 80 W. Michigan, five years after that store shut and two years after you reopened a block away.

Somebody searching for Pastrami Joe’s in Battle Creek is being told you are out of business.

Sirved also says you are closed on Saturday. Six sources, six answers, and no authoritative one to correct them against, because your own site publishes none.

Google does not know you are a restaurant.

Your homepage carries structured data, and all of it is generic. WebPage, ImageObject, BreadcrumbList, WebSite, Organization. There is no Restaurant type, no LocalBusiness, no address, no telephone and no opening hours anywhere in it.

That is the machine-readable half of the hours problem. Even if you typed the hours onto a page tomorrow, nothing on the site tells a search engine they are opening hours.

Your homepage source

Your Battle Creek page is titled “battlecreek”.

The browser tab, the Google result and every shared link for that page read battlecreek - Pastrami Joe’s. Lowercase, no space, the URL slug used as a title.

It also has no meta description, so Google writes its own from whatever text it finds. Your catering page has no description either.

The Battle Creek page · The catering page

Two names, two navigations, one unexplained relationship.

The Battle Creek section is branded Little Joe’s, runs a different menu bar from the rest of the site, has its own contact form and its own menu page, and nothing anywhere explains how the two names relate.

Its body copy is a word for word duplicate of the homepage paragraph. The addresses are typed by hand onto ten separate pages, so a correction is ten edits and one of them always gets missed.

There is no page on your site where somebody can order.

The nav sends people straight off to pastramijoesmarshall.hrpos.heartland.us, Battle Creek goes to a different Heartland subdomain, and pastramijoes.com/online-ordering/ is a 404.

So there is nowhere on your own domain that answers “can I order right now, and from which counter”. The customer leaves your brand at the exact moment they decide to spend money.

The catering page, meanwhile, prices everything down to the cookie tray. So the site can do it. The retail menus just do not.

What we could not check

Both Heartland ordering pages refuse automated access, so we have not seen the ordering experience itself, what it looks like, or what it charges. Everything above about ordering is about your website, not about Heartland.

Facebook, Instagram and Yelp all block automated reading. We know the accounts exist and we know @pastramijoes is linked from nowhere on your site, and that is all we know. The Yelp review counts and the CLOSED flag come from search result titles, not from the pages.

We have not measured your site on a real phone for speed, and the Google star ratings we saw came through an aggregator rather than from Google. We are not making a claim about either.

The part that matters most

Your website already broke at two locations. You are planning a third.

You told Choose Marshall you want to open a bakehouse and expand to more Michigan stores. Look at what happened to the website when you went from one store to two: it cloned itself. A second navigation, a second contact form, a second menu page, a second brand name, and the homepage paragraph copied into it word for word.

What two stores cost you

Ten hand-typed copies of two addresses. Two menus that have already drifted apart in small ways nobody intended. Two ordering links and no page that offers both. One brand with two names and no explanation.

What a third would cost

On this architecture, a third clone. A third nav, a third form, a third menu to keep in step with the other two, and fifteen copies of three addresses. Every hour change becomes a hunt. The work grows with every store you open.

What we build instead

One file holds the locations. Add a bakehouse to it and the site grows a location page, a set of hours, a footer entry, a sitemap entry and a Google listing on its own. Opening store three is one edit, not one rebuild.

The second thing that matters

Right now, ordering online means leaving your website.

Tap Order Online Marshall on your site and you land on pastramijoesmarshall.hrpos.heartland.us. Battle Creek goes somewhere else again. Neither address has your name in it, neither page has your hours on it, and there is no page anywhere that offers both counters. The one thing your website is for hands the customer to somebody else's domain.

Where your prices actually live

Inside Heartland. That is why your own menu page has none: fifty-five of your sixty-five items show no price on pastramijoes.com. Your prices are real, they are just somewhere a customer has to go looking for them, on a page you do not control.

What we built instead

Ordering on pastramijoes.com. Pick a counter, build the order, pay, done. It knows which counter is open, it stops taking orders twenty minutes before close, and it takes catering bookings with a real total instead of a form that promises a phone call.

Your register does not move

Heartland keeps the till, the drawer and the card machine. Nobody behind the counter retrains. What changes is the online channel, which is the part that currently sends your customers away from your own name.

What it costs you: nothing a month

No monthly fee for ordering, ever. The guest pays a 99¢ order fee at checkout, the same way every ordering platform charges one. Half of it comes back to you. We keep 49¢. That is the whole business model and there is nothing else in it.

A screen for the counter

Orders arrive on a screen with a chime. Start it, done, and the ticket clears. Sold out of the chili? One tap and nobody can order it. Slammed? Pause online orders for twenty minutes and it turns itself back on.

You edit your own menu

Prices and items live in a text field you own, not in a vendor's back office. Change a price at 6am and the site has it at 6am. Send us the sandwich prices and the whole board turns on the same day.

What is live on the demo today: catering, soup and the seasonal board, because those are the only prices you publish anywhere. Every number on it came off your own pages. We did not guess a single sandwich price, and we would rather show you an honest half than a made-up whole. Try it.
